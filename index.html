01 — Reference

Voltage drop

Every conductor has resistance, so voltage is lost as current travels down its length. Too much loss and lights dim, motors labor, and electronics misbehave — even though the panel is delivering full voltage.

Single-phase VD = (2 × K × I × L) / CM
Three-phase VD = (1.732 × K × I × L) / CM
  • K — resistivity constant: 12.9 for copper, 21.2 for aluminum
  • I — load current, amperes
  • L — one-way conductor length, feet
  • CM — conductor cross-section, circular mils

Recommended limits

PEC/NEC 210.19(A) and 215.2(A), Informational Note, recommend keeping total voltage drop within:

3% branch circuit alone
3% feeder alone
5% combined, feeder + branch

These are advisory design targets, not hard violations by themselves — but exceeding them is treated as a design deficiency in practice, and some AHJs enforce them as mandatory.

03 — Reference

Short circuit / fault current

Every breaker and panel has an interrupting rating — the maximum fault current it can safely clear. PEC/NEC 110.9 and 110.10 require that rating to meet or exceed the fault current actually available at that point in the system.

  1. Available fault current at the transformer — derived from transformer kVA and percent impedance: Ifault = (IFLA × 100) / %Z
  2. Reduce it for conductor impedance — the point-to-point "f-factor" accounts for how much the feeder or branch conductor itself limits fault current: f = (1.732 × L × I) / (C × N × V) for three-phase
  3. Apply the multiplier — M = 1 / (1 + f), then Idownstream = Iupstream × M
  4. Compare to breaker AIC rating — the interrupting rating at that point must be ≥ the calculated available fault current
Point-to-point f-factor f = (1.732 × L × I) / (C × N × V) — three-phase
f = (2 × L × I) / (C × N × V) — single-phase

C is a conductor constant from standard point-to-point tables (varies by size and material); N is the number of parallel conductors per phase. The values used in the calculator below are typical reference figures — confirm against the current PEC/Bussmann point-to-point tables before finalizing a real design.

06 — Reference

Illumination

The lumen method estimates average illuminance across a room from luminaire output, room area, and how efficiently the space uses that light.

Average illuminance E = (N × F × UF × LLF) / A
  • E — average illuminance, lux (lm/m²)
  • N — number of luminaires
  • F — lumen output per luminaire, lm
  • UF — utilization factor (fixture + room reflectance efficiency), typically 0.4–0.7
  • LLF — light loss / maintenance factor, typically 0.7–0.85
  • A — room area, m²

Typical target levels

Common residential design targets — confirm against project requirements or the current PEC illumination guidance:

100–150 lx bedroom
150–300 lx living / dining
300–500 lx kitchen
150–200 lx bathroom
100–150 lx garage / utility
300–500 lx reading / study

These are typical reference targets, not code minimums — verify against project specs or the current PEC before finalizing a design.
